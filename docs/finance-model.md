# Finance Model

Aureon Systems Group is modelled as a multinational enterprise whose operating activity is converted into balanced accounting entries before any financial statement is produced.

## Business divisions

Software combines recurring subscriptions and services. Revenue growth is driven by customer base, pricing, renewal behaviour and commercial momentum. Its economics are primarily gross-margin and headcount led.

Hardware is a physical-product business supplied by manufacturing entities. Revenue is driven by units, price, mix and product lifecycle. Manufacturing cost, capacity, inventory, transfer pricing and CAPEX are explicit parts of the model.

Events & Projects recognises project-based revenue with timing and mix variability. Project volume, duration and margin profiles differ from recurring and product businesses.

Spare Parts operates as an aftermarket business. It is driven by installed base, SKU demand, price, inventory coverage and obsolescence risk.

## Management P&L

The management P&L is structured as:

Net Sales

less Variable Production Cost

less Variable Selling Cost

equals Marginal Contribution

less Fixed Production Cost

equals Gross Profit

less Operating Expenses

less Depreciation

equals EBIT

less Interest

equals EBT

less Tax

equals Net Income

Marginal Contribution and Gross Profit are both retained because they answer different management questions. Marginal Contribution measures the economics of incremental commercial activity. Gross Profit adds the fixed manufacturing burden required to operate the production footprint.

## Working capital

Trade receivables are created by external sales and released by customer collections. Trade payables are created by external procurement and operating expenses and released by supplier payments. Inventory is generated by physical-product flows and therefore interacts with manufacturing, intercompany transfers, product demand and cash.

The analytical layer derives DSO, DIO, DPO and the cash conversion cycle from the ledger-based balances and operating activity.

## Intercompany and transfer pricing

Factory entities manufacture Hardware and Spare Parts for commercial entities. Transfers are invoiced using an 8 percent manufacturing cost-plus policy.

The legal books therefore contain intercompany revenue, cost of sales, receivables and payables. Consolidation removes reciprocal P&L and balance-sheet positions. Any transfer-pricing profit remaining in unsold group inventory is removed from consolidated inventory and retained earnings until the inventory is sold externally.

## CAPEX

Capital investments are modelled as projects rather than a percentage-only dashboard assumption. Projects have approved amounts, legal-entity ownership, spend profiles and status. Construction-in-progress is transferred to PPE when appropriate and depreciation follows the asset lifecycle.

## Financing and tax

Debt, interest and tax are generated through the accounting engine. The resulting cash movements are included in financing and operating cash flow rather than being inserted directly into the cash statement.

## Product portfolio decisions

Products have a lifecycle. The portfolio engine evaluates profitability and commercial performance and can create phase-out decisions and replacement launches. Those events modify future operating activity instead of acting as annotations on historical results.

## Forecasting

Every monthly close creates forecast vintages for Base, Upside and Downside scenarios. Historical vintages remain available for forecast accuracy and bias analysis. Forecast generation is driver-based and incorporates historical forecast error without using future actual information.

## Financial controls

The release gate validates journal balance, trial balance, legal balance sheets, reciprocal intercompany balances, consolidated balance sheet, cash-flow reconciliation, consolidation bridges and forecast look-ahead. A failed control prevents publication.
